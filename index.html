Compromise Checker: clear results to act on

A tool that checks Linux servers for signs of a break-in. It answers one question: has this server been broken into? It isn't a vulnerability scanner or a patching tool. It logs in over SSH, runs a read-only collector, and brings the evidence back to your own machine. The tool writes nothing to the server. Each server gets an estimated chance of a break-in, with the evidence behind every finding and what to do about it. It's built for small organizations that can't afford commercial security products, and it shows how we handle AI in sensitive work: the optional AI advisor explains results and suggests next steps but never acts. Names and addresses are replaced by default before anything is sent, and a model on your own machine is an option. Open source under AGPL. Pre-release 0.1.2 is out: how to install it.

NouriLoop: AI designed in from the start

A nutrition-tracking web application. AI reads meals from photos, turns a goal described in your own words into a measurable one, and explains glucose responses. A person reviews every estimate before it's saved. Underneath, cost stays a choice: the application runs on Anthropic, on any of the models available through OpenRouter, or on your own Ollama server; simple tasks go to smaller models automatically; and every AI call is metered, so the cost of each feature is known, not guessed. We design client systems the same way. No organization should be locked into one AI vendor's prices.

It was built mostly with AI coding agents, with every step planned, directed and reviewed by a person. That's the process our developer course teaches. Open source under AGPL, and live at app.nouriloop.com.
