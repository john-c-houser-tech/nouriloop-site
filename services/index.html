What we do

We help organizations put AI to work carefully. That means clear expectations for staff, a sound plan for what to build, and an experienced developer for the systems that need work. You work with John directly, from the first conversation to the final report.

Help your staff use AI tools well

In most organizations, staff are already using AI tools. What's missing is a shared understanding of what's allowed, what "done" means, and who's accountable. We fix that with a short session for mixed audiences and a one-page AI Use Standard tuned to your existing policies, which your team can adopt when you're ready.

You keep: the AI Use Standard, a Quality Procedures checklist, and the session materials.

Plan AI work that fits your budget and your data

Which tasks AI should take on, which model is right for each, and what it will cost every month. Cost is one reason to choose a model. Where your data goes is often the bigger one. Some information can go to a commercial AI service under the right contract terms. Some shouldn't leave your organization at all, and for that a model can run on your own hardware. We sort your work into those categories, choose models to match, and test cheaper or local models before trusting them. Then we set up metering so the monthly bill is predictable. Our development process and tools work the same way with any of these models, so the choice stays yours, and you can change models when prices, terms or your rules change.

You keep: a written plan with each task, where its data may go, the model chosen and why, and the expected monthly cost.

Help your developers use AI agents without losing control

AI coding agents produce more code than any person can review, so the work needs a written process: plan approval, tests shown, a second review, a person at the merge. We bring that process and a starter kit of skills that travel between projects, and leave your team running both.

You keep: a starter skill library and an instructions-file template, installed in your own repositories.

Retire the technical debt you've been living with

Old applications, undocumented scripts, the system only one person understands. An experienced developer rebuilds and documents them, with AI help that lowers the cost of work that used to be too expensive to start. The work happens under your review and on your repositories. We can also add AI features to applications you already run.

You keep: the code, the documentation and the history, all in your own repositories.

Make the next vulnerability a routine update, not an emergency

Old, unmaintained applications are the easiest targets, and the tools attackers use to find weaknesses now work for defense too. We find and fix vulnerabilities in your applications and the libraries they depend on, bring outdated frameworks and language versions up to date, and set up automated checks that warn you when a new vulnerability affects your code.

You keep: the patched code, a record of what was fixed and why, and automated security checks running in your own repositories.

A four-week adoption pilot

The easiest way to start is one department, four weeks:

  1. Week 1: we read your existing policies, talk with the people who'll use the tools, note what data your staff work with, and draft your AI Use Standard.
  2. Week 2: a session for staff introduces the tools and the standard.
  3. Week 3: your team works under the standard. A second session, for developers or a follow-up for everyone depending on what week 2 showed.
  4. Week 4: we review what was produced against the standard and send a written report: what's working, what to change, and what to do next.

You keep: the standard, the quality procedures, both sessions' materials and the report.

What we don't do

We don't run your servers or networks. Systems administration stays with your staff. We work alongside them. We're not a security firm: we don't do penetration testing or run security monitoring.

Questions we get

Do you work with public agencies?

Yes. Most of John's 37 years were in library and public-sector systems, most recently in county IT. We're based in Astoria and work in person across Clatsop County and the Pacific Northwest, and online with anyone whose working day overlaps ours. Our training aligns with your existing policies rather than replacing them.

What is an AI Use Standard?

A one-page document that says what's allowed, which classes of data may go to which tools, when AI use must be disclosed, and when a person must review the result. We draft it from your existing policies, so it adds to them rather than replacing them, and anyone on your team can edit it. It comes with the Working with AI Tools session and with the four-week pilot.

Can our data stay inside our organization?

Yes. Some information can go to a commercial AI service under the right contract terms, and some shouldn't leave your organization at all. For the second kind, a model can run on your own hardware. We sort your work into those categories, choose models to match, and test the local ones before trusting them.

Is it safe for staff to use ChatGPT or similar tools with our information?

It depends on the information and on the tool. The same model can be safe in one tool and not in another, because the terms differ on what the service keeps and what it trains on. That's why the AI Use Standard sorts your data into classes and says which may go where. Until you have one, treat anything you wouldn't post publicly as off limits.

How do you keep AI coding agents from introducing errors?

With a written process that checks the work at each stage: the plan is approved before coding starts, tests are shown, the code is reviewed again, and a person is at the merge. We bring a starter kit of skills and an instructions-file template, install them in your repositories, and leave your team running it. Our own applications were built this way, with every pull request reviewed before it was merged.

Which AI vendor do you use?

Whichever fits the job. Our process and tools work the same with Anthropic, with the models available through OpenRouter, or with a model on your own hardware through Ollama. We choose by cost and prioritize protection of your intellectual property. We meter every call so the monthly bill is predictable, and you can change models when prices, terms or your rules change. No organization should be locked into one AI vendor's prices.

What do we own when the project ends?

Everything. The code, the documentation and the history live in your own repositories, under your review, from the start. Training leaves you the AI Use Standard, the Quality Procedures checklist and the session materials, all editable. Nothing depends on us to keep running.
